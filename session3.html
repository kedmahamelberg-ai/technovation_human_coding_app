Quick codebook

Code how the post represents people and AI. Use only what the post states or clearly implies. Do not decide whether a claim is true, add missing information, or give your own opinion.

Answer the two questions separately. A post can describe an effect on people without describing an outcome for the AI system, or the reverse.

You can reopen this guide from the top-right button at any time during practice and all six coding sessions.

1. What happens to people?

Choose this explanationMeaning
People use AI to improve something they already doAn existing task or ability becomes faster, easier, better, or possible at a larger scale. Example: preparing an estimate in seconds instead of days.
People gain a new ability or possibility through AIThe post describes a genuinely new capacity, perspective, learning opportunity, creative path, or form of participation.
People have less control, privacy, access, or choice because of AIThe post describes or clearly anticipates surveillance, manipulation, dependence, lock-in, refusal, privacy risk, or loss of agency.
People lose skills, livelihood, creativity, identity, or well-being because of AIThe post represents an actual diminishment or damaging human outcome, such as lost work, weakened skills, poorer thinking, or harm to well-being.
No clear change for peopleNo enabling or constraining human experience is stated or clearly implied. A headline, event, feature list, or AI mention can be neutral when it does not describe a human change.

2. How is the AI system represented?

Choose this explanationMeaning
The post describes the system as working or producing a useful resultA concrete function, output, or performance is represented as reliable, useful, successful, or effective.
The post describes growth in data, users, learning, profit, adoption, or reachThe central claim concerns development of the model or growth of its operator or ecosystem—for example, added training data, more users, profit, adoption, or market reach.
The post describes AI-related access or action as blocked, refused, limited, or constraining peopleThe represented action or access is refused, censored, gatekept, withheld, banned, or limited—or the system’s operation constrains people through surveillance, manipulation, or dependence.
The post describes system failure, poor output, deterioration, or broader harmThe post represents errors, hallucinations, unreliability, worsening quality, harmful synthetic content, misinformation, fraud, stereotypes, or other damaging outcomes.
No clear outcome for the AI system or ecosystemAI may be mentioned, questioned, advertised, credited, or discussed, but no clear function, growth, restriction, failure, or damaging outcome is represented.

Fast decision rules

  • Existing task improved → people improve something they already do. Genuinely new capacity or path → people gain a new ability or possibility.
  • Less autonomy, privacy, access, or choice → people have less control. Actual loss of skill, livelihood, creativity, identity, or well-being → people lose something.
  • A product or feature advertisement does not automatically establish a human experience. A concrete claimed function or output can still establish that the system is represented as working.
  • “Smarter,” “better,” “faster,” or a useful completed output concerns system performance. Use the growth choice only when data, learning, profit, users, adoption, or reach is explicit and central.
  • “AI was tested” with no result, an event announcement, an unrelated #AI tag, or a general question about AI usually gives no clear system outcome.
  • For AI-generated content, look at the wording: a concrete useful output supports system performance; an explicit new creative path can support a new human possibility; a bare credit or hashtag alone may remain neutral.
  • If several signals appear, choose the clearest main representation in the post. Do not invent a secondary effect.
  • Do not ask what AI wants, feels, or deserves. Judge only the behavior or outcome that the post attributes to the system, operator, or ecosystem.
